SŪCHELFOR THE PAGE

Forty-one lines ready to letter — every one composed from the grammar, not around it

Hails, bridge chatter, curses, prayers, and the full drive-litany. Each entry gives the line, pronunciation, morpheme gloss, translation, and a usage note for the panel. Three small grammar pieces surface here that the main codex left implicit — and one of them is a theorem: commands carry no mood and no anchor, because an order asserts nothing, so it escapes the duty to conjugate the truth. In Sūchel, the imperative is the only way to speak without taking a position.

IMPERATIVE

Bare stem + -u. No mood, no anchor — non-assertive. Prohibitive: vo + stem-u. jed-u! "go!" · vo jed-u! "don't go!"

VOCATIVE

Particle ō before the noun: ō mān! "hey, crewmate!" With entangled kin, the suffix does the warmth: ō Sōrnmai "O my drive."

QUESTIONS

Final particle vu (from *wu, SC-3). Polar only; answers are ver "true" or vo "not." …ver-a=ka vu? "is it so?"

ACCUSATIVE FORMS

Object clitic -(e)n: en "I" → enen "me"; ish "you" → ishen. Denominal verbs are licit fleet-talk: osh "mouth" → oshu! "swallow (it)!"

A HAILS & PARTINGS 挨拶

Meeting and leaving

Fleet greeting logic: you greet with truth, and you never say "goodbye" — you say surface again. Partings differ by whether the other is staying inside beacon coverage or leaving it.

01
ver ish-ol.
[ver iˈʃol]
truth 2SG-DAT
"Truth to you." — Hello.
Use: the standard hail, any register. Neutral, safe, universal.
02
ver ishmai-ol.
[ver iʃˈmai.ol]
truth 2SG.ENT-DAT
"Truth to you, kin." — the warm reply.
Use: answering 01 with the entangled form upgrades the greeting to intimacy. Answering a stranger this way is forward; answering crew any other way is cold.
03
ō mān!
[oː maːn]
VOC crew.hand
"Hey, mate!"
Use: attention-getter across a deck or a bar. Friendly-rough.
04
kad ishen tan-u.
[kad ˈi.ʃen ˈta.nu]
beacon 2SG.ACC hold-IMP
"Beacon keep you."
Use: parting, when the other stays inside coverage. The default farewell of settled folk.
05
zukad-eth, lesh ishen tan-u.
[ˈzu.ka.deth · leʃ ˈi.ʃen ˈta.nu]
dark.time-LOC alignment 2SG.ACC hold-IMP
"In the dark, may fair phase keep you."
Use: parting for someone leaving coverage. Where no beacon can vouch, only lesh — alignment, luck — remains to invoke. Solemn.
06
hōl-u ret.
[ˈhoː.lu ret]
surface-IMP again
"Surface again." — the divers' goodbye.
Use: between divers, always. Saying "goodbye" (any parting of 04/05 type) to a diver about to dive is a faux pas verging on omen; you tell them to come back up.
07
en jed-a=mi.
[en dʒeˈda.mi]
1SG go-T=PROP
"I'm off — by my own clock."
Use: casual leave-taking. The =mi anchor is the shrug: my time, my business.
08
men-u, mān.
[ˈme.nu maːn]
wait-IMP crew.hand
"Hold on, mate."
Use: stopping someone mid-turn; softening bad news to come.
B BRIDGE CHATTER 艦橋

Commands and responses

Orders are bare and anchor-less (they assert nothing). Reports are conjugated — a report without mood and anchor is not a report, it is noise. Call lines marked ◆, responses ◆.

09
nuv jed-u!
[nuv ˈdʒe.du]
DOWN go-IMP
"Take her down!"
Use: the dive order. Remember motion verbs demand a depth word — the command is two syllables of pure tower-grammar.
10
hau jed-u!
[hau ˈdʒe.du]
UP go-IMP
"Bring her up!"
Use: the surfacing order; also shouted as an abort.
11
jel id-u.
[dʒel ˈi.du]
seam open-IMP
"Open the seam."
Use: the crossing order. By fleet custom it is spoken quietly, never shouted — you do not shout at the seam.
12
Sōrnmai, tan-u.
[ˈsoːrn.mai ˈta.nu]
drive.ENT hold-IMP
"Hold, my Sōrn."
Use: murmured to the engine under strain. The entangled -mai on one's own drive is universal among pilots; the drive is kin, not property.
13
kad ver-a=ka vu?
[kad veˈra.ka vu]
beacon speak.true-T=BEAC Q
"Does the beacon hold?" — Are we in coverage?
Use: the standard nav query after any maneuver.
14
ver-a=ka.
[veˈra.ka]
true-T=BEAC
"It holds." — Lock confirmed.
Use: the good answer. Two words that mean we exist in shared time.
15
vo ver-a=ka. zukad.
[vo veˈra.ka · ˈzu.kad]
NEG true-T=BEAC · dark.time
"No lock. We're dark."
Use: the bad answer. The single word zukad as a sentence is fleet shorthand for we are beyond all vouching.
16
tem tan-u, eni om.
[tem ˈta.nu · ˈe.ni om]
pattern hold-IMP · 1PL all
"Hold the pattern, all hands."
Use: pre-crossing order. tem here is both the ship's formation and, ominously, the Κ5 word for what may or may not survive.
C CURSES & OATHS 呪詛

Swearing like a crosser

Fleet profanity is cosmological: you curse by the hazards. Mild oaths invoke scars; strong ones invoke the mouths. The oath-formula swears on one's own clock — gravest precisely because no beacon can back it.

17
jelvos!
[ˈdʒel.vos]
seam.scar
"Scar!" — Damn it.
Use: the everyday expletive — for things gone subtly wrong (instruments lying, readings off). A scar is where the vacuum lies to you.
18
velosh ishen oshu!
[ˈve.loʃ ˈi.ʃen ˈo.ʃu]
Formless.mouth 2SG.ACC swallow-IMP
"May a Formless mouth swallow you!"
Use: the strong curse — fight-starting in any port bar. Invoking the mouths at someone is wishing them the one fate with no verdict.
19
vo-tem!
[ˈvo.tem]
NEG-pattern
"Shapeless!" — you incoherent waste.
Use: insult for incompetence. To have no tem is to be the thing that couldn't even survive a crossing in principle.
20
kru jel-eth!
[kru ˈdʒe.leth]
blood seam-LOC
"Blood at the seam!"
Use: oath of dismay/horror — invokes failed condensation, the smeared crossing. Said when witnessing disaster.
21
an vo hōl-u!
[an vo ˈhoː.lu]
3SG NEG surface-IMP
"May he never surface!"
Use: the vicious curse for an enemy diver — the exact inversion of farewell 06. Unforgivable between crew.
22
gal ishen tan-u!
[gal ˈi.ʃen ˈta.nu]
the.still 2SG.ACC hold-IMP
"May the Still hold you!"
Use: pilot's curse — gal is the becalmed vacuum, the Dead-Sea. Wishing someone held motionless in structureless calm: a sailor's hell.
23
vurel-en chel!
[ˈvu.re.len tʃel]
madman-GEN speech
"Madman's talk!"
Use: dismissal of nonsense — with built-in dramatic irony, since the Madman's Theorem is the one thing that could break the trap. Every use is a tiny act of the consensus.
24
en ver-a=mi: …
[en veˈra.mi]
1SG speak.true-T=PROP
"I swear by my own clock: …"
Use: the oath-formula. Swearing on =mi instead of =ka means: this holds even where no beacon vouches for me — the gravest oath precisely because it renounces all external backing. Breaking a =mi oath makes one vo-tem.
D PRAYERS & BLESSINGS 祈り

What they say to the Tower

Two registers: Assembly liturgy (addressed to the beacon and the Tower, conjugated in confident T=ka) and crossers' folk-piety (addressed to the seam and the lost, living in T• and =zu). The grammar is the theology.

25
Turmai, enen tan-u.
[ˈtur.mai ˈe.nen ˈta.nu]
Tower.ENT 1SG.ACC hold-IMP
"O our Tower, hold me."
Use: the universal short prayer — the entangled -mai claims kinship with the whole hierarchy. Said at need, by everyone, believers or not.
26
kad om-ol ver-u.
[kad ˈo.mol ˈve.ru]
beacon all-DAT speak.true-IMP
"Beacon, speak true for all."
Use: Assembly versicle, opening services. The congregation is praying for shared time itself.
27
jel lesh-eth id-u.
[dʒel ˈle.ʃeth ˈi.du]
seam alignment-LOC open-IMP
"Seam, open in fair phase."
Use: the pre-crossing prayer — whispered by crew as the drive spins up. Companion to order 11.
28
enmai nexath-mai tan-a=mi.
[ˈen.mai ˈne.ksath.mai taˈna.mi]
1PL.ENT gap-ENT hold-T=PROP
"We keep our gaps."
Use: the crossers' creed. Note the grammar's confession: gaps take entangled possession — the holes in a life are kin, not property. Said at reunions, over drink, before dives.
29
an zu jed-t-a=mi.
[an zu dʒedˈta.mi]
3SG adrift go-PFV-T=PROP
"She has gone adrift." — She is dead.
Use: the death formula. Plain T — a fact — but anchored =mi: only her own clock knows when. The fleet does not say "died"; it says the anchor was lost.
30
hōl-eshe=zu, maiel.
[hoːˈle.ʃe.zu ˈmai.el]
surface-T•=DARK kinsman
"Surface — seam-true — in the dark, kinsman."
Use: said over the unreturned. The funeral verb is conjugated T•: their surfacing can be neither asserted nor denied, so the mourning mood is the self-dual one. The most theologically loaded two words in the language.
31
ver enmai-eth ve-a=ka.
[ver enˈmai.eth veˈa.ka]
truth 1PL.ENT-LOC be-T=BEAC
"Truth is among us."
Use: Assembly congregational response — the confident register: plain T, beacon-anchored, truth located inside coverage.
32
eshe-ver men-a=zu.
[ˈe.ʃe.ver meˈna.zu]
seam.true-truth wait-T=DARK
"The Tragic Truth waits in the dark."
Use: heretical folk-prayer — whispered by crossers' widows, never in Assembly hearing. A T-mood assertion about the T• truth, anchored where no one can check.
E THE DRIVE-LITANY 機関の典礼

Call and response, engine-room, before and through a crossing

The full sequence as spoken — engineer calls ◆, crew responds ◆. It runs the crossing's own ΛL arc: orders (anchor-less) → reports (=mi) → the seam (T•) → the one word inside → existence reasserted on proper time → the beacon reacquired. Letter it across a page-turn and the grammar does your pacing.

"My Sōrn, hold breath."
Use: litany opening — the engineer addresses the drive as kin and stills it.

"Down, down, to the seam."

"We are running under."
Use: the vertigo confirmed — the crew names the desync as it takes them.

"The seam opens — seam-true."

— (the interval that has no sentence)
Use: spoken aloud by all hands at the crossing — the only word said inside, and it is the word that is not a word: the crew pronounces the gap so the gap will have been theirs. Letter as its own black panel.

hōl-t-eshe=zu vu?
[hoːlˈte.ʃe.zu vu]
surface-PFV-T•=DARK Q
"Surfaced — seam-true, in the dark — yes?"
Use: the far-side call. Even the question of arrival must be asked in T•, in dark time.
40

[hoːlˈte.ʃe.zu · ˈen.mai veˈa.mi]
surface-PFV-T•=DARK · 1PL.ENT be-T=PROP
"Surfaced. We are — by our own clock."
Use: the Resurrection response. The crossing stays T•; but existence itself is reasserted in plain T — anchored only to proper time, until the network is found again. We are, even if no one can vouch when.
41
kad ret ver-a=ka.
[kad ret veˈra.ka]
beacon again true-T=BEAC
"The beacon holds again."
Use: reacquisition — the litany's release of tension. The first =ka since line 33: the ship has rejoined shared time. Letter it small, after silence.
LETTERING · THE GAP

Give ne (38) a full black panel or a thin vertical stroke-glyph between panels. Never letter dialogue inside a crossing — the grammar forbids it, so the page should too. The reader learns the rule by feeling it.

LETTERING · MOODS AS COLOR

A workable convention: T plain white lettering · T⁻ cool tint · T⁺ warm tint · T• red or double-stroked balloons. Readers will track the theology by color before they ever parse a gloss.

LETTERING · ANCHORS AS TAILS

=ka lines get normal balloon tails; =mi lines a tail to the speaker's chest (their own clock); =zu lines no tail at all — words unanchored, floating in the panel.

REGISTER CHEAT-SHEET

Assembly speaks in T=ka (confidence). Pilots live in =mi (self-reliance). Grief and heresy live in T• and =zu. If a character's arc bends, bend their morphology one clitic at a time.
