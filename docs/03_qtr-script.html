NAVCHERTHE FLEET SCRIPT

Ship-carve: the stencil hand of the fleets — angular, featural, and with exactly one curve in the whole system

From nav "ship" + cher "to carve, write" (*ker- → SC-1). Old Pelagic wrote the flowing tide-script, boustrophedon — lines alternating direction "as the tide runs." The fleets linearized it left-to-right for terminals and stencils, demoting the old vowel diacritics to small inline letters. What follows is the full working system: seventeen consonant letters, five small vowels with the held-breath bar, and the operator sigils that let a verb's mood and anchor be carved in two strokes.

DESIGN RULE 01 · FEATURAL

Letterforms encode phonetics: head position = place (labials head-left, alveolars head-center, velars head-right); a foot-stroke = voicing; a slash = frication. Learn six letters and you can predict the rest.

DESIGN RULE 02 · STENCIL-SAFE

Every stroke is straight; nothing encloses fully; the script cuts clean from a plate. The only curve in the entire system is the =zu anchor — the unanchored mark is the one sign that refuses to close.

DESIGN RULE 03 · TWO HANDS

Careful hand spells every suffix phonetically — liturgy, law, log-of-record. Bridge hand compresses mood and anchor into sigils — instrument panels, hull marks, haste. Same letters, two registers.

01 THE CONSONANT LETTERS 子音字

Seventeen letters, one logic

Sixteen living consonants plus he — the threshold letter. In Sūchel, /h/ survives only at the start of a word (hōl, hau); everywhere else it died into vowel length (SC-4). So its letter is drawn as a stem with a gap in it — the letter that is mostly absence.

02 THE VOWELS 母音

Small letters and the held-breath bar

Vowels sit half-height between the consonants — the fossil of their diacritic past. Length is the held-breath bar drawn above: a vs ā is one stroke of patience.

03 THE OPERATOR SIGILS 記号

Grammar you can carve in two strokes

Because every finite verb must carry mood and anchor, bridge hand compresses them into sigils. The five moods are variations on one square; the seam-mood T• is the filled square — the seam-mark. The three anchors: the beacon's pulse, the self's chest-stroke, and the dark's open hook — the script's single curve.
