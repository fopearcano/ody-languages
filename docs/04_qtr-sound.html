THE SOUNDOF SŪCHEL

Phoneme key · the four-rule stress algorithm · every phrasebook line, playable as rhythm

For the letterer, the reader-aloud, and any future voice. The core discovery codified here: the veridical mood suffix seizes the stress — in Sūchel you can hear the truth-claim, because the mood carries the beat. And since commands assert nothing, imperatives are beatless: flat, stem-stressed, quiet. Every line below has a rhythm player (synthesized beats: reliable) and a rough voice (your browser's speech engine on an English respelling: an approximation, clearly labeled as one).

02 THE STRESS ALGORITHM 強勢

Four rules, applied in order

Run these top to bottom; the first that applies wins. Rule 4 is the one that makes Sūchel sound like nothing else — and it is ideology made audible.

RULE 1
Compounds stress their first member (on that member's own stressed syllable). The second member is subordinated. NUV-ran · JEL-mar · SŌRN-mai · E-she-ver · ZU-kad
RULE 2
Finite verbs: the mood seizes the stress. Stress the syllable containing the veridical suffix's first vowel — the truth-claim carries the beat. Anchor clitics (=ka =mi =zu) are weightless and never stressed. ve-RA-ka (ver-a=ka) · ta-NA-mi (tan-a=mi) · hōl-TE-she-zu (hōl-t-eshe=zu) · i-DE-she-mi (id-eshe=mi)
RULE 3
A long vowel seizes the stress in any other word. SŪ-chel · HŌ-lu · MĀN · ĀN
RULE 4
Otherwise: stress the penult of the stem — and case suffixes never shift it once set. I-dre-nes · PEL-gar · VU-rel → VU-re-len (gen.) · ZU-kad → ZU-ka-deth (loc.) Corollary — imperatives are beatless: -u adds no beat, commands stay flat. TA-nu, JE-du, I-du: an order asserts nothing, so nothing in it can carry the truth-beat.
