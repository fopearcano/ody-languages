SŪCHELTHE CROSSING-SPEECH

A full language built the way languages are really built — a proto-tongue, seven sound changes, and a grammar that cannot say a crossing without conjugating the truth of it

Real languages grammaticalize what their speakers cannot afford to be vague about. A civilization that lives with five-valued logic, beacon-anchored time, and un-narratable crossings would build those into its verbs — not its dictionary. Sūchel [ˈsuː.tʃel] is that language: daughter of Old Pelagic, tongue of the fleets, with four grammatical systems no human language has. Every canon name — Sōrn, Idrenes, Pelgar — is derived, not decorated.

01 WHO SPEAKS IT 話者

Two tongues, one family

Peterson's first rule: a language is its speakers. Sūchel did not fall from the sky — it eroded out of an older tongue, the way all living languages do, and the split tracks the civilization's own history across the seam.

THE ANCESTOR · LITURGICAL

Old Pelagic · Pelgar

The tongue of the pre-crossing civilization — the surface era, when the deep (*pelag-, "the open deep") was still only ocean and sky. Rigid SOV, fully inflected, phonologically conservative. When the first crossings shattered the old cosmology, the Pelagian Assembly froze this language as its liturgical and doctrinal register: catechism, verdicts, and law are pronounced in Old Pelagic to this day. Speaking doctrine in the old tongue is a claim: the truth has not moved since before the seam.

THE DAUGHTER · FLEET VERNACULAR

Sūchel · *suw-kel "crossing-speech"

Two dozen generations of fleet life eroded Old Pelagic into Sūchel — the working language of divers, crossers, and beacon-keepers. Same bones, worn smooth by seven regular sound changes; but where the Assembly's tongue stayed still, Sūchel grew new grammar for the new world: truth-moods for the five values, time-anchors for the three clocks, depth-words for the tower, and a particle for the gap no verb can enter. The pilot argot ("running under," "dark time") is its slang register, calqued below.

CANON DERIVATION 01

Sōrn — the drive

*sow-orn "thread-instrument" (o-grade of root *suw-/*sow- "to thread through, cross" + instrumental *-orn) → SC-3 glide loss with lengthening → sōrn. The engine is literally "the threader." Its zero-grade sister *suw-kel gives the language's own name — drive and tongue are cognates.

CANON DERIVATION 02

Idrenes — the bridge

*id- "open" + *ren- "path" + *-es "place of" = *ideren-es "place of the opened path" → SC-2 syncope → Idrenes. So "Idrenes Bridge" is a pleonasm, like "Sahara Desert" — a translation habit of surface-siders, which fleet speakers find quietly funny.

CANON DERIVATION 03

jel — the seam 𝔍

*gel- "to stitch, to join" → SC-1 palatalization before front vowel → jel /dʒel/ — which is why the scholars' symbol is 𝔍. Derivatives: jelmar "seam-pearl," jelvos "seam-scar," jelsīl "seam-crosser." The universe's deepest object wears the humblest word: a tailor's.

04 CORE GRAMMAR 文法

The bones

Verb-final (SOV), agglutinative, postpositional — inherited straight from Old Pelagic. Nouns are simple; the verb carries the world on its back.

NOUNS

Case, number, and entangled possession

Number: plural -i (sīl "crosser" → sīli). Case clitics: accusative -(e)n, genitive -en, dative -ol, locative -eth.

Entangled possession: Sūchel splits possession the way the substrate splits correlation. Alienable things take genitive -en; but kin, one's ship, and one's gaps take the entangled suffix -mai — possession as shared amplitude, not ownership. You say kel-en "my words" but nav-mai "my-entangled ship." To use -en of your own ship is to announce you plan to sell her.

enmai1PL.ENT"we" — crew-we, the entangled we. Distinct from eni, the mere plural. A captain who says eni of her own crew is understood to be resigning.
VERBS

The template

STEM – (ASPECT) – MOOD = ANCHOR

Aspect: perfective -t-, imperfective unmarked. Mood: one of the five veridical moods (next section) — obligatory. Anchor: one of the three temporal clitics — obligatory on finite verbs. Negation is the particle vo before the verb. Pronouns: en 1SG, ish 2SG, an 3SG (+ -i plural, -mai entangled).

an sū-t-eshe=zu3SG cross-PFV-T•=DARK"She crossed" — and the verb alone confesses: seam-true, in time no beacon reached.
05 THE FOUR SYSTEMS 物理の文法化

Where the cosmology lives in the verb

Quechua marks evidence; Guugu Yimithirr marks compass direction. Sūchel marks what its world makes unignorable: the truth-value of the limit, the anchor of time, the direction of depth, and the place where narration fails. These four systems are the language's reason to exist.

SYSTEM 01 · ΛL IN THE VERB

Every finite verb conjugates for the ΛL status of its proposition. There is no mood-less verb — you cannot speak without taking a position on the truth.

The temporal anchors

Every finite verb must declare which time it happened in. Using the wrong anchor is not style — it is lying, and fleet courts treat it as perjury.

SYSTEM 03 · THE TOWER IN MOTION

Depth directionals

Like Guugu Yimithirr's compass, Sūchel motion is tower-absolute. Verbs of movement are ungrammatical without a depth satellite: nuv "down-tower, deeper" (*nub-) or hau "surfaceward" (*haw-). "Go" bare is baby-talk; adults always know which way the tower lies.

nav nuv ran-eshe=miship DOWN run-T•=PROP"The ship runs deeper" — whence the pilot slang nuvran, "running under": the dive-vertigo itself, lexicalized.
SYSTEM 04 · THE GAP IN SYNTAX

The particle ne

It is syntactically forbidden to place a verb inside the narration of a crossing. The interval held at T• never became memory; the grammar refuses to pretend otherwise. The particle ne (*nex- "absence") stands where the verb cannot. In fleet orthography it has its own mark — a short vertical stroke — which your page can render as a glyph: panels of ne.

en jel-eth tel-t-eshe=ka. ne. en hōl-t-eshe=zu.1SG seam-LOC reach-PFV-T•=BEAC. GAP. 1SG surface-PFV-T•=DARK"I reached the seam. — . I surfaced, in dark time." The life between the two sentences has no sentence.

The Tragic Truthwhat actually happens · unsayable-as-fact

The only honest conjugation, and the trap closes: T• is self-dual, so the sentence and its negation have the same value. Spoken truth that cannot be asserted over its denial. The epistemic trap is not in the debate. It is in the verb.
